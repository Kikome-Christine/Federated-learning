Financial regulators need machine-learning oversight of data that data-residency and confidentiality rules
prevent them from centralising. Federated learning keeps raw data at each institution, but a federation spanning
resource-constrained edge nodes and cloud nodes must still decide, every round, \emph{which nodes may and
should participate}. We present a regulatory-aware, resource-aware orchestration framework in which residency
and licensing eligibility is a hard filter, evaluated per round through an Open Policy Agent (OPA) policy,
applied before a composite capability--bandwidth--latency--availability score. We implement the selection and
coordination path in Rust and evaluate it against two policy-blind baselines and a centralised floor across
a deterministic simulator, a \texttt{tc/netem}-shaped container testbed, and a
[k3s / Raspberry~Pi] deployment including one trial over a real [mobile-data] link, with \NumReps{} independent
repetitions per configuration. The policy-aware selector admitted [0] ineligible participants
in [N] rounds, whereas the policy-blind baselines violated policy in [X\%] of rounds when a quarter of nodes were ineligible;
